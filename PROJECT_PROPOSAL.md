# CIT One — Institutional Pilot Proposal

**Title:** CIT One: AI-Assisted Campus Grievance, Infrastructure Monitoring and Transparency Portal  
**Proponent:** Student project team, Department of Computer Science and Engineering, CIT Kokrajhar  
**Requested initial decision:** Permission to demonstrate a prototype and run a limited, supervised pilot  
**Status:** Proposal only; no institutional approval or funding is implied.

## 1. Executive summary

CIT One is a proposed single portal for students to report campus facility issues, track progress and view anonymised service-level information. It covers roads and pathways, electricity, water and drainage, hostels and sanitation, classrooms and laboratories, Wi-Fi/IT, safety concerns and other facilities. AI assists with categorisation and duplicate/urgency suggestions; authorised staff retain decision-making responsibility.

The prototype is designed to be built and demonstrated by a student team before requesting institutional support. The first stage requires no claim of government funding and no integration with official systems.

## 2. Need and intended outcomes

The project aims to make the reporting process easier to find, reduce duplicate reports, help authorised staff prioritise issues, give students a reference ID and status history, and provide aggregated evidence for infrastructure planning. The system complements rather than replaces existing official grievance and emergency channels.

## 3. Pilot scope

**Included:** student accounts, complaint submission, optional photo evidence, unique IDs, private complaint detail pages, basic AI-assisted category/priority suggestions, an administrator queue, status history, public anonymised dashboard, email draft generation and pilot evaluation.

**Excluded from initial pilot:** automatic official complaint filing, unsupervised email sending, government funding applications, procurement, payment processing, automatic sanction/approval claims, emergency dispatch and production integration with institute systems.

## 4. Roles and governance

- **Student reporter:** submits a good-faith report and follows status.
- **Pilot administrator:** reviews and routes reports only after formal authorisation.
- **Department contact:** provides inspection and progress information.
- **Faculty supervisor / project lead:** oversees technical quality and student team.
- **Institute sponsor / data owner:** approves purposes, access, retention, publication and escalation.

Before launch, the institute should nominate an accountable owner, approved complaint categories, the receiving departments, escalation rules, privacy notice, data retention period and a process for correcting false or abusive reports.

## 5. Privacy and transparency

Public views should show aggregated counts and carefully moderated issue summaries only. Reporter names, email addresses, full descriptions, attachments, sensitive locations and private staff notes should remain restricted. Public resolution status should only be marked verified when a designated authorised person has confirmed it. Avoid publishing allegations about identifiable individuals or sensitive security details.

## 6. Delivery plan

| Phase | Work | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery | Meet student representatives, faculty and relevant administrators; map current channels | Requirements and approval owner documented |
| 2. Prototype | Demonstrate login, complaint submission, status tracking and dashboard | Core workflow passes test cases |
| 3. Privacy/security review | Test access controls, uploads, account roles and public views | Written issues list and mitigations |
| 4. Supervised pilot | Small opt-in student cohort; use non-sensitive test/approved reports | Pilot feedback and operational metrics collected |
| 5. Evaluation | Review accuracy, usefulness, response times and privacy incidents | Evaluation report and go/no-go decision |
| 6. Institutional expansion | Only after written approval, deploy on approved infrastructure | Named owner, support plan and approved budget |

## 7. Proposed pilot metrics

- Number of valid reports and percentage with sufficient location/detail.
- Time from submission to acknowledgement and first staff update.
- Percentage assigned to a responsible unit.
- Time to verified resolution, reported by category.
- AI category suggestion agreement with human reviewers.
- Duplicate suggestion precision after manual review.
- Student satisfaction and accessibility feedback.
- Number of privacy/security incidents (target: zero).

Do not claim improvements until the pilot provides baseline and post-launch data. Do not use complaint counts alone to rank departments because reporting volume may reflect awareness rather than performance.

## 8. Budget and support request

**Stage 1 — Student prototype:** use existing student laptops and open-source software where permitted. No external funding is assumed. Any expenses should be approved under the applicable student-project process before purchase.

**Stage 2 — Supervised pilot:** request a written decision on approved hosting, a domain if required, institutional email/identity integration, security review, backups and staff time. Obtain quotations or applicable institute rates before preparing a budget.

**Stage 3 — Scale-up:** only after a successful evaluation, prepare a detailed estimate for hosting, maintenance, support, accessibility, security and training. The competent institute authority must determine the permissible funding source and approve expenditure under applicable rules.

No funding amount is stated because costs, procurement rules, scheme eligibility and institutional requirements have not yet been verified.

## 9. Approval requested

The student team requests:

1. permission to demonstrate the prototype to the faculty supervisor and designated institute representatives;
2. nomination of a faculty mentor and potential administrative contact;
3. guidance on existing grievance channels and how this project should complement them;
4. review of privacy, security and accessibility requirements;
5. if the prototype is acceptable, permission to conduct a limited supervised pilot using approved data and infrastructure;
6. consideration of institutional support only after the pilot evaluation and detailed cost estimate.
